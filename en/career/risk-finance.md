# Risk, Finance & Vendor Governance

Security leaders are regularly asked two questions: "How much risk are we taking?" and "Why does this cost so much?" This article connects them. ## Risk appetite and tolerance

**Risk appetite** is the amount and type of risk an organization is willing to take in pursuit of its goals. **Risk tolerance** describes the acceptable variation around that appetite. Ideally, senior leaders agree these in plain language and your decisions refer back to them; facilitating that conversation is part of leadership.

Two complementary views help:

| View | Beginner-friendly example | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualitative | agreed levels such as low / medium / high, each defined | Definitions must be shared, or the labels mean nothing |
| Quantitative | ranges, scenarios, or estimated impact and likelihood | Every number rests on assumptions; show them |

There is no single universal formula that converts every risk into money. Use ranges, state assumptions, and be honest when evidence is thin.

## Governance artifacts you can show

- **Risk register**: risk, owner, causes, current controls, assessment, treatment decision, residual risk, review date.
- **Treatment plan**: what will change, who pays, target date, and how progress is tracked.
- **Exception or waiver log**: who accepted a risk, for how long, with what compensation.
- **Key risk indicators**: a small set of signals that tell you whether the risk profile is shifting.

A register updated once a year is a museum, not a management tool.

## Budget cycles and value framing

Learn your organization's budget calendar: discovery, draft, approval, execution, and review. Distinguish **CAPEX** (a one-time build or purchase) from **OPEX** (subscriptions, people, and ongoing operations); a "cheap" platform can be expensive to run, and the reverse is also true. Multi-year roadmaps help because security value often arrives after the first invoice.

Frame value as risk reduction, business enablement, and obligations the organization has chosen to meet. Avoided loss is an estimate, not a promised saving. Vendor ROI calculators and efficiency percentages are **marketing claims** with their own assumptions, not established facts. This is education, not financial, tax, or investment advice.

## Vendor governance

Before signature: understand the supplier's security posture, the scope of any certification claim, data flows, subcontractors, hosting locations, and past incidents. In the contract and **SLA**, look for incident-notification duties, service levels, audit rights, data return or deletion, and exit assistance.

Watch **concentration risk** — several critical services depending on one cloud, region, or provider — and remember that third-party risk remains your organization's risk. An **exit plan** should answer: how would we operate if this supplier failed, changed terms, or was acquired? Is access revoked and data recovered when a contract ends? Governance frameworks such as those discussed on the companion [CSIX COBIT site](https://siedysoes.github.io/csix-cobit/) and control practices on the [CSIX ISO 27001 site](https://siedysoes.github.io/csix-iso27001/) offer useful structuring ideas.
