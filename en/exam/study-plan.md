# A Practical Study Plan

CCISO v4 — AI-Enhanced is an executive credential, so memorising definitions is not enough. This unofficial plan builds the habit that matters: turning security facts into decisions, documents, and short briefings. It is a **suggested routine**, not an official EC-Council programme. Check the current blueprint and policies on [eccouncil.org](https://www.eccouncil.org/train-certify/certified-chief-information-security-officer-cciso/), [cert.eccouncil.org](https://cert.eccouncil.org/), and [ciso.eccouncil.org/cciso-certification](https://ciso.eccouncil.org/cciso-certification/). We publish no official courseware or exam dumps; all names are trademarks of their owners.

## Before week 1

Confirm your route with [Eligibility: Full CCISO & Associate Path](eligibility.md), download the current blueprint, and write down *why* you are studying (a job target, a current role, or a promotion). Keep a simple notebook or folder with four tabs: blueprint, artifacts, weak domains, and questions.

## An eight-week example

| Week | Focus | Artifact you write |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Five domains and what "govern to strategize" means | One-page domain-to-experience map |
| 2 | Govern: policy hierarchy, roles, accountability | Policy outline with owner and review date |
| 3 | Lead: stakeholders, communication, culture | Stakeholder map and a 5-line briefing |
| 4 | Operate: risk and incident lifecycle | Short risk register with likelihood/impact |
| 5 | Secure: controls, architecture, cryptography basics | Control-to-control-objective table |
| 6 | Strategize: budgets, metrics, vendor decisions | One-page budget memo with two options |
| 7 | Case studies across all domains | Two short case analyses with trade-offs |
| 8 | Blueprint review and legitimate practice | Weak-domain list and revised study notes |

Repeat any weak week before moving on. The artifacts are yours — short, honest, and useful at work.

## Study methods that stick

- **Spaced repetition:** make flashcards for key terms, then revisit them after a day, a few days, and a week. Short reviews beat one long cram.
- **Writing practice:** explain a risk in 150 words to a non-technical manager. If you cannot explain it simply, you do not understand it yet.
- **Case studies:** read public, reputable incident reports and ask: what governance failed, what was communicated, what would I have funded?
- **Track weak domains:** keep a table with *domain / topic / confidence / next action*. Review it every week, not only before the exam.
- **Teach-back:** discuss a topic with a colleague and note the questions you cannot answer.

## Practice exams: the right way

Use sanctioned sample questions, official practice products, or scenarios you write yourself. Score them by topic, not only by total. **Avoid exam dumps**: they can violate certification policies, become outdated, and create the illusion of learning without building real leadership skill.

## A realistic weekly rhythm

| When | Activity |
|---|---|
| 3 short sessions | Blueprint topic + notes |
| 1 longer session | Write or revise one artifact |
| 1 short review | Flashcards and weak-domain table |

Adjust the rhythm to your job and family commitments; consistency matters more than intensity. If you travel or study in a second language, add extra reading time for official terminology.

## Keep it honest
