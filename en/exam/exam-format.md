# CCISO Exam Format & Blueprint

Many beginners search the internet for "how many questions" and "what score is passing." We will not give you a fixed number here. EC-Council states that exam and certification policies may change without notice, and figures copied from old blogs can be wrong. This unofficial guide shows **how to find the truth** and turn it into a study plan. Verify everything on [eccouncil.org](https://www.eccouncil.org/train-certify/certified-chief-information-security-officer-cciso/), [cert.eccouncil.org](https://cert.eccouncil.org/), and [ciso.eccouncil.org/cciso-certification](https://ciso.eccouncil.org/cciso-certification/) before scheduling anything.

## What not to treat as fixed fact

Do not rely on second-hand claims about exam **duration, number of questions, passing score, price, retake rules, or CPD numbers**. The issuer can update any of them. If a page gives precise figures without a date and an official source link, close the tab. Vendor statistics on certification pages are marketing claims with their own methodology — not promises about your result or salary.

## Why the official blueprint matters

The exam blueprint (also called an exam outline or candidate guide) is the issuer's map of the assessment. It describes domains, topics inside them, and how the exam is structured. At executive level, that map is more useful than memorising trivia: it shows where leadership judgment is expected.

Use it to:

- **Allocate time** — focus on areas the current document says carry more weight.
- **Set a vocabulary baseline** — learn the official terms for governance, risk, and strategy.
- **Build artifacts** — turn each blueprint line into something you can write: a policy outline, a risk entry, a board slide.
- **Measure readiness** — mark each topic as *know / shaky / unknown* and revisit the unknowns.

## A simple blueprint workflow

| Step | Action | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Download the current blueprint from an official EC-Council page | Dated PDF or page saved in your notes |
| 2 | Split it into the five domains: Govern, Lead, Operate, Secure, Strategize | A working checklist, not a memorised list |
| 3 | Add one real work example per topic you can | Experience map for your application |
| 4 | Rate confidence and schedule review | Weak-domain list |
| 5 | Re-check the blueprint just before booking | Confirm nothing changed |

If the current document differs from what a training provider says, the issuer's document wins. Ask the provider which version they used.

## Legitimate practice, not dumps

Use sanctioned sample questions, official practice exams, or your own scenario writing. **Exam dumps and "brain dumps" are a bad deal**: they can violate certification policies, they go stale, and they do not teach you to brief a board or approve a budget. Dumps train recall of someone else's answers; the exam and the job reward judgment.

## Exam-day practicalities

Check the issuer's current instructions for identity requirements, online proctoring environment, allowed materials, scheduling windows, accommodations, and result reporting. Rules differ by delivery mode and region. Test your connection and workspace early, but confirm the rules only through official channels.

## After the exam

Whatever the outcome, record what you learned and update your weak-domain list. If you do not pass, read the **current** retake policy from the issuer instead of trusting an old forum post. Renewal and continuing education expectations are covered in [Costs, Validity & CPD](cost-validity.md) — again, without copied numbers.

This article is educational, not legal or financial advice. Confirm every exam detail with EC-Council.
